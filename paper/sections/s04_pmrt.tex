\section{Proposed framework}
\label{sec:method}

The proposed framework has three components (Figure~\ref{fig:idea}). The referee randomises its accept-or-reject
decisions and logs their propensities, which turns the controller's logs into an experiment with a known design. The
predictable matched-filter randomisation test (PMRT) uses this design to test each hypothesis of
Section~\ref{sec:estimand} and returns a signed causal effect map with asymptotic control of the FDR. A map-driven
referee then uses the map to reject harmful requests, and an edge-certification gate restricts it to rejections whose
side effects on the guard KPIs are certified small. This section describes the three components in turn and states
what each of them guarantees.

\subsection{Test statistic}
\label{sec:pmrt}
\label{sec:pmrt_idea}

In logs written by a reactive controller, the network state drives both the decision and the KPIs
(Figure~\ref{fig:idea}a). When the referee draws each mode with a logged propensity, the state no longer determines
the decision (Figure~\ref{fig:idea}b), and the propensities define what chance alone would produce. A KPI that moves
with the modes more than the propensities allow is then evidence of an effect, even when the propensities themselves
depend on the network state. PMRT measures this co-movement with a statistic that pairs a score of each unit's mode
with a weight built from the KPI series, and compares it with the values obtained when the modes are redrawn from the
logged propensities (Figure~\ref{fig:idea}c). The test establishes that an effect exists, not how large it is.

Take a family $f$ and its units $u$ in information order: episode, opening time, cell. With $L(\text{accept}) = 1$ and
$L(\text{reject}) = 0$, the assignment score of unit $u$ is
\begin{equation}
v_u = \mathrm{sgn}_u \bigl( L(W_u) - \pi_u \bigr),
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sgn}_u$ is the direction of the opening request. Over the policy's draw, $v_u$ has mean zero and
variance $\pi_u(1 - \pi_u)$, so an acceptance that was likely scores little and one against the odds scores more.
The weight $w_u$ summarises how the KPI under test moved after the unit opened. It subtracts from the KPI a ridge
prediction~\cite{hoerl1970ridge} built only from what was known at opening, so that the prediction contains none of
the effect of the unit's own mode. A matched filter then combines the remaining residuals over the receiving cells and
the seconds after opening into one number, favouring the bins where the training data showed more effect and less
noise. The weight is then centred on the running mean of earlier units and clipped at a scale predicted at
opening~\cite{huber1964robust}. Every fitted part was trained on earlier episodes and fixed before any evaluation data
were generated; Algorithm~\ref{alg:pmrt} and Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} in Appendix~\ref{app:proof} give the
construction step by step.

The validity of the test rests on one property of the weight: apart from the KPI series under test, it uses only
information fixed before the unit drew its mode. It therefore uses no later unit, no unit of another family opening at
or after $u$, and no mean or slope over an episode's realised units, because whether those units exist can depend on
$W_u$. An earlier construction that centred the score over each episode's realised units broke this rule. Because a
rejected request is proposed again about 60~s later, that centre depended on earlier modes, and on null outcomes its
rate of $p \le 0.05$ for sleep hypotheses was 0.14 against the nominal 0.05. Placebo logs missed the failure, because
applying every request removes the re-proposal. The statistic and its
variance are
\begin{equation}
S = \sum_u v_u w_u, \qquad
\sigma^2 = \sum_u \pi_u (1 - \pi_u)\, w_u^2, \qquad
z = S / \sigma,
\label{eq:stat}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^2$ is the variance of $S$ over redraws of the modes with the weights held. Since it is the same for every
redraw, $z$ is normalised by the design variance rather than studentised. The reported sign is that of $S$ times an
orientation $o$, the sign of the training effect over 0--90~s, which is set only if the training $z$ of that effect is
at least 2 in magnitude. Where $o$ is unset, the reported sign is that of the plain total of the residuals over 0--90~s.

\subsection{Inference and false discovery rate control}
\label{sec:inference}
\label{sec:declaration}

The modes of family $f$ are redrawn $B = 9999$ times from the logged propensities, with every other family fixed and
the weights held, and $z_b$ is recomputed for each redraw. Each hypothesis has a two-sided p-value $p$ and, where $o$
is set, a one-sided value $p_d$ for each effect direction $d \in \{+1, -1\}$:
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{1 + \#\{b : |z_b| \ge |z|\}}{B + 1}, \qquad
p_d = \frac{1 + \#\{b : d\, o\, z_b \ge d\, o\, z\}}{B + 1},
\label{eq:pval}
\end{equation}
where $\#$ counts redraws. A hypothesis is tested only when it has at least 30 units, 5 accepted, 5 rejected and 3
episodes. The sharp null $H_0(f, r, k)$ of Section~\ref{sec:estimand} states that the outcome a unit would have at any cell
and opening time is the same under any assignment of $f$'s modes. It is stated over possible units, because which
units are realised depends on the modes. The history $\mathcal{G}_{u-1}$ holds all other randomness and the modes of $f$'s
first $u - 1$ units (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_setting}), and the result rests on four assumptions.

\begin{description}
\item[(A1) Design.] For every unit $u$, its existence, cell, opening time, direction $\mathrm{sgn}_u$ and propensity
  $\pi_u$ are $\mathcal{G}_{u-1}$-measurable, $\Pr(W_u = \text{accept} \mid \mathcal{G}_{u-1}) = \pi_u$, and
  $\pi_{\min} \le \pi_u \le 1 - \pi_{\min}$ for a fixed $\pi_{\min} > 0$.
\item[(A2) Sharp null.] $H_0(f, r, k)$ holds.
\item[(A3) Predictable weights.] Every $w_u$ is $\mathcal{G}_{u-1}$-measurable.
\item[(A4) Regularity.] As the number of episodes grows, the design variance $\sigma^2$ grows without any single unit
  dominating $S$ (Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}).
\end{description}

In operational terms, (A1) requires the RIC to draw each mode with the propensity it logs, set from the past and kept
away from 0 and 1. Assumption (A2) is the hypothesis under test and asks nothing of the RIC. Under (A3), the analyst,
not the RIC, builds each weight from what was known when the unit opened, apart from the KPI under test. Assumption (A4)
requires enough logged units that none dominates $S$; in the simulator it holds when the clipped weights stay bounded
and $\sigma^2 / n$ settles to a positive limit.

\begin{proposition}[Asymptotic validity of PMRT]
\label{prop:pmrt}
Assume (A1) to (A3). Then $S = \sum_u v_u w_u$ is a martingale with respect to $(\mathcal{G}_u)$, and its predictable
quadratic variation is $\sigma^2$. Let (A4) also hold and the number of episodes grow.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The statistic $z = S / \sigma$ converges in distribution to $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.
\item Conditionally on the data, every redraw $z_b$ has mean 0 and variance 1, and its distribution function converges
  uniformly to $\Phi$ in probability.
\item For every fixed $B$ and every $t \in [0, 1]$, $\limsup \Pr(p \le t) \le \lfloor t(B + 1) \rfloor / (B + 1) \le t$.
  The same holds for $p_d$ for each $d$ when $o$ is fixed in advance.
\end{enumerate}
The p-values $p$ and $p_d$ are therefore asymptotically valid. The value $p$ differs from
$2\{1 - \Phi(|z|)\}$ only by a term that vanishes in probability and by Monte Carlo error.
\end{proposition}

Only the normal limit is asymptotic: the martingale property, and $\sigma^2$ as its predictable variance, hold exactly
in every sample. The redraw p-value is nevertheless not exact in finite samples, placebo logs included, because which
units exist, their propensities and their weights all depend on earlier draws of the family
(Appendix~\ref{app:proof_exact}). For the same reason the exact randomisation test for sequential experiments of
Bojinov and Shephard~\cite{bojinov2019timeseries} does not apply here. A sharp null on one KPI does not fix the
network state that creates the units and sets the incumbent's propensities, so the adaptive design cannot be replayed.

The 60 hypotheses of one analysis are declared together by a weighted Benjamini--Yekutieli (BY)
step-up~\cite{benjamini2001dependency,blanchard2008fdr} at $q = 0.05$. Hypothesis $j$ enters with a weight $\omega_j$
and, where a prior direction $d_j$ is set, with $p_{d_j}$, and otherwise with $p$; weights and directions are derived
from the training episodes alone~\cite{roeder2009weighted} (Table~\ref{tab:pipeline}). A declaration is a rejected
hypothesis with its sign, $d_j$ where the one-sided value was used and otherwise the reported sign.

\begin{corollary}[Asymptotic false discovery rate]
\label{cor:fdr}
Let $\mathcal{H}_0$ hold the true sharp nulls among the $m = 60$ hypotheses. Let (A1) to (A4) hold for each of them,
and let the weighted step-up use weights $\omega_j \ge 0$ with $\sum_j \omega_j = m$ and directions $d_j$ fixed from
data independent of the evaluation data. Then, whatever the dependence between the p-values,
\begin{equation}
\limsup_{n \to \infty} \mathrm{FDR} \le \frac{q}{m} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{H}_0} \omega_j \le q,
\label{eq:fdr}
\end{equation}
where a false discovery is the rejection of a true sharp null.
\end{corollary}

Untested hypotheses count in $m$ and are never declared, which is conservative. The proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\subsection{Scope of the guarantees}
\label{sec:reading}

A declared edge states that the modes of family $f$ change KPI $k$ somewhere in the cells that relation $r$ covers,
and the FDR bound covers that claim asymptotically. It does not cover the sign: the reported sign is the direction of
the weighted association between the centred modes and the outcome, which need not be the sign of $\tau_{\pi}$. Nor
does it cover which relation carries the effect. A unit's neighbour and far cells are the own cells of other units of
$f$, so an effect of $f$ on its own cells also moves the neighbour and far sums. The hypotheses $H_0(f, \text{nbr}, k)$ and
$H_0(f, \text{far}, k)$ are then false, and declaring them is correct. Whether PMRT attributes edges to the same
relation as the ground truth is therefore an empirical question, and the far hypotheses are no negative control.

\subsection{Map-driven referee}
\label{sec:acting}
\label{sec:slope}
\label{sec:referee}

The referee needs the size of each effect, not only its sign, and takes it from the design slope $\hat\beta$, which
regresses each unit's centred outcome on its assignment score. Let $e_u = y_u - \tilde y_u$ be the outcome minus an
outcome centre $\tilde y_u$ computed from earlier units' outcomes and $u$'s pre-window. Under the randomised policy,
\begin{equation}
\hat\beta = \frac{\sum_u v_u e_u}{\sum_u v_u^2}
          = \frac{1}{N} \sum_u \mathrm{sgn}_u \Bigl( \frac{\mathbf{1}\{W_u = \text{accept}\}\, e_u}{\pi_u}
            - \frac{\mathbf{1}\{W_u = \text{reject}\}\, e_u}{1 - \pi_u} \Bigr),
\label{eq:slope_ht}
\end{equation}
with $\pi_u = 0.5$, which is the Horvitz--Thompson estimator of $\tau_{\pi}$ and converges to it
(Lemma~\ref{lem:slope}). Under a state-dependent policy such as the incumbent, it converges to the
$\pi_u(1 - \pi_u)$-weighted average of $\mathrm{sgn}_u \tau_u^{\pi}$. The lemma assumes an outcome centre fixed before
$u$'s mode is drawn, whereas the implemented centre also uses earlier outcomes whose windows are still open; with a
strictly predictable centre, recomputed post hoc, no slope changed sign (Appendix~\ref{app:x16}).

A causal effect map $M$ gives each declared edge $(f, r, k)$ a signed coefficient $\beta(f, r, k)$. For PMRT it is the
declaration's sign times the magnitude of the design slope; where the two signs disagree, the declaration's sign is
kept and the disagreement counted. An associational map uses the method's sign times the magnitude of the
least-squares slope of the outcome on the logged mode, oriented by the request's direction. The referee decides when a
decision unit opens, on the opening request's family, direction and context, and later requests of the pair inherit
the mode. A request opens a unit only if its parameter last changed longer ago than the actuator's minimum interval
between changes, and a rejecting unit rejects only requests in its opening direction. For a request of family $f$ and
direction $d$, the referee applies four rules, with the same constants for every map:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Effects}. It sums the direction-adjusted effects $\Delta_k = d \sum_r \beta(f, r, k)$ over the own,
neighbour and far relations for each KPI $k$.
\item \emph{Harm}. The request is judged on the first KPI with $\Delta_k \ne 0$ in the order protected-slice
violations, service violations, RLFs. It is harmful if that $\Delta_k$ is positive, and is otherwise accepted.
\item \emph{Rejection}. The request is costly if $\Delta_e > 0$ for energy. Its pressure is the largest protected-UE
below-floor fraction over the own and neighbour relations through which it is harmful, or zero without such a
relation or report. A harmful request is rejected if it is costly or its pressure exceeds 0.05.
\item \emph{Guard conflict}. A rejection is guard-conflicted when accepting would help the first nonzero guard KPI
among service violations and RLFs. Such a unit is rejected only if the previous unit of the same cell and family was
accepted, so that a guard conflict delays a request by at most one unit.
\end{enumerate}
The constants were fixed before any evaluation run, but the structure of the rules was developed on earlier results.

\subsection{Edge-certification gate}
\label{sec:gate}

A map leaves some guard edges undeclared, and a referee acting on it can harm a guard KPI through such an edge. The
gate prevents this by letting the referee reject a class of requests only when every undeclared guard edge of the
class is certified small. It checks every class $(f, d)$ that the referee can reject against the own, neighbour and far
relations of the two guard KPIs, service violations and RLFs. Rejecting a request of direction $d$ moves a guard KPI
by $-d\,\beta$, so a rejection's harmful direction is $h = -d$. We write the class $(f, +1)$ of requests that raise $f$
as $f$-up, so that sleep-up holds the requests to put a pico to sleep, and $(f, -1)$ as $f$-down.

A guard edge that the map declares stays under the referee's own logic. An undeclared edge is certified when the
one-sided 90~\% upper bound of $h\,\beta$ from the map's own estimator is at most a tolerance $\varepsilon(f, d, k)$,
and is otherwise unresolved; a class with any unresolved edge is always accepted. PMRT's bound uses the design slope
with its martingale standard error, the associational maps' bound the least-squares slope with an independent-error
standard error, and the oracle map's a conservative bound from its stored 95~\% interval; a map without an effect
estimator cannot certify. The tolerance is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(f, d, k) = \frac{0.05\, G_k}{3\, \max\{N(f, d), 1\}},
\label{eq:tol}
\end{equation}
with $G_k$ the mean per-episode total of guard KPI $k$ and $N(f, d)$ the mean number of units of the class per
episode, both measured under accept-all on 40 development episodes (Appendix~\ref{app:simparams}). Suppose that every unit of a
class were rejected and that each rejection moved the KPI by at most $\varepsilon$ on each of the three relations. The
guard ratio would then move by at most 0.05, half the margin of 0.10 that a referee may use under the 1.10 limit of
Section~\ref{sec:estimand}. The certificate is thus a first-order budget per edge, not a bound on the referee's guard
ratios: it ignores overlapping windows, classes acting together and declared edges, so whether a referee meets its
guards is checked on held-out seeds. The uncertified classes depend only on the map, the bounds and $\varepsilon$, and
were fixed before the evaluation data were generated.
