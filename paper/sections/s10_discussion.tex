\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support two findings of different strength. The validity of a test that uses the logged design follows from
its construction and was confirmed on placebo logs, on null outcomes and in controlled environments with known causal
graphs. The finding that a valid map did not improve safe conflict mitigation, in contrast, rests on a single simulated
scenario and a single referee rule.

\subsection{Implications for causal discovery}
\label{sec:disc_discovery}\claim{EMP-POWER}

The validity of the test derives from the logged design rather than from a model of the network, which makes it the
property most likely to transfer to other networks. The randomisation distribution is generated from the logged
propensities alone, whether uniform or state-dependent, so the guarantee holds for any statistic, weighting or prior
direction fixed before each unit draws its mode. The controlled environments indicate that the guarantee belongs to the
design rather than to PMRT, since partial correlation adjusted for the design variables also retained its nominal rate
under a logged confounded policy.

Statistical power, in contrast, depends on the choice of statistic: design-adjusted partial correlation reached the
higher recall in the latent-confounding environment, and PMRT on the randomised simulator logs
(Section~\ref{sec:study4}). The matched filter and weights of PMRT therefore increase power rather than validity, and a simple
design-adjusted test provides the same guarantee.

\subsection{Implications for conflict mitigation}
\label{sec:disc_mitigation}

In the evaluated scenario, the safety guards rather than the accuracy of the map limited safe mitigation: with the gate,
the PMRT referee made the same decisions as the never-sleep rule, and the oracle map performed no better
(Section~\ref{sec:disc_decision}). Further improvements in discovery would therefore not have increased the benefit of
safe mitigation here. Conversely, the ungated result shows that a single missed edge was sufficient for the referee to
breach a guard limit, so a map should not drive mitigation decisions without a step that certifies the effect of each
decision on every guard KPI. Whether the guards rather than discovery limit safe mitigation in other scenarios, with
other guard sets and objectives, cannot be determined from a single scenario.

\subsection{Deployment requirements}
\label{sec:disc_deploy}\claim{EMP-X3}\claim{EMP-S1-STRICT}\claim{EMP-X4}

The test requires a conflict-mitigation layer that draws each accept-or-reject decision with a known propensity and
records that propensity exactly. A policy that changes over time does not violate this requirement, provided that each
logged propensity is the one with which the decision was drawn. A recorded propensity that differs from the true one is
a misspecified design: in the controlled environments, PMRT-GBM and PMRT-Lin exceeded the nominal null-edge rate when
the assumed dither width was 0.8 times the true width, and every PMRT variant when the assumed strength of the logging
policy was twice the true value (\esm{esm:envs}). The RIC should therefore record each propensity at the moment of the
draw, from the code that performs it, rather than reconstruct it afterwards, and keep propensities bounded away from 0
and 1.

Randomised logging imposes two costs on the operator. First, randomisation rejects some requests that a fixed rule would
accept, and random rejection reduced recovery below that of accept-all (Section~\ref{sec:disc_decision}). Second, evidence accumulates
at the rate of decision units, each a 60~s mode followed by a 90~s outcome window, rather than at the rate of the RIC's
control loop; the smallest samples evaluated in the simulator, 60 episodes of 600~s, correspond to hours of network
time. At that size, small p-values occurred at their nominal rate under a stricter significance threshold
(\esm{esm:discovery}), although on the small-effect nulls of the ground truth a rate of up to three times nominal could not
be excluded. The logging time required in a deployed RIC depends on its traffic and decision rate, which this study
did not examine.

For operators who apply learned discovery methods to designed logs, a placebo check provides an inexpensive screening
step. The pipeline is applied unchanged to an additional column drawn from the logged design, which by construction
affects no KPI, and any edge declared from that column disqualifies its declarations as causal evidence. Passing the
check is not sufficient (Section~\ref{sec:study4}), so positive causal evidence still requires a test whose validity
follows from the logged design.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limits}\claim{EMP-X15}\claim{EMP-X16}

The simulator results come from one scenario, one incumbent policy and one referee rule, and none of them involves a
real RIC, a testbed or a compliance claim. The controlled environments model no deployed RAN, and no method evaluated
in them used the matched filter and weighted BY procedure of the PMRT applied in the simulator; their claims hold for
the dithered-setpoint design tested, and their evidence against design-blind tests rests in one environment on a
placebo edge only. Results from a single block of seeds carry seed-level heterogeneity larger than their intervals
suggest (\esm{esm:decision}).

The validity of PMRT is asymptotic rather than exact, even on placebo logs. The ground truth uses a single 90~s horizon
with every other unit accepted, so it targets $\tau_{\mathrm{AA}}$ rather than $\tau_{\pi}$, and the labels of material
effects, and with them the precision of PMRT, depend on the threshold $\delta_k$. The FDR bound applies to the edge, not
to the relation to which the edge is attributed. The design slope and the bounds of the gate rest on an approximately
predictable outcome centre; a strictly predictable centre changed the map slopes by \val{x16.map.rel_min} to
\val{x16.map.rel_max}~\% without a change of sign, moved the bounds by a median of \val{x16.gate.median_pct}~\% of
their tolerance and altered no certification (\esm{esm:decision}). No guard covers the protected slice, and the gate
bounds of the associational maps assume independent errors, so they can be too narrow on dependent KPI series by an
amount that this study did not quantify.
