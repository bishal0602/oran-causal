\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

Table~\ref{tab:related} places the proposed approach among the method families of three areas: causal inference from
logged data, conflict maps in O-RAN, and safe conflict mitigation.

\subsection{Causal effects from feedback-driven logs}
\label{sec:related_design}

When a controller produced the logs, an edge test draws its validity either from assumptions about the
data-generating process or from a known assignment mechanism. RIC logs leave the assumptions untestable, because
requests react to the state that drives the KPIs, and no O-RAN work we found takes the mechanism from the RIC's own
decisions.

Observational causal discovery rests on assumptions. Granger causality~\cite{granger1969causal} and the
Peter--Clark (PC) algorithm~\cite{spirtes2000causation} assume that no common cause goes unmeasured, NOTEARS fits an
acyclic structural equation model~\cite{zheng2018notears}, and the randomized conditional correlation test
(RCoT)~\cite{strobl2019rcot} and PCMCI~\cite{runge2019pcmci} test conditional independence.

Design-based inference takes its validity from the assignment mechanism instead: when the assignment probabilities
are known, redrawing the assignments under a sharp null, which states that the assignment changes no unit's outcome,
gives a p-value with no model of the outcomes~\cite{imbens2015causal}. Such tests reach sequential experiments on a
single time series~\cite{bojinov2019timeseries}, switchback experiments~\cite{bojinov2023switchback} and
response-adaptive designs, where re-running the adaptive assignment rule keeps the type I error only under
conditions~\cite{simon2011randomization}. The conditional randomisation test keeps any test statistic
valid~\cite{candes2018knockoffs}, and Benjamini--Yekutieli (BY) control keeps the false discovery rate (FDR) of many
tests under arbitrary dependence~\cite{benjamini2001dependency}. Under known interference, design-based
estimators~\cite{aronow2017interference} and randomisation tests~\cite{basse2019interference} also exist, and in
networks A/B tests of congestion control are biased when the arms share a
bottleneck~\cite{spang2021unbiased}, which CausalSim addresses with randomised trial data~\cite{alomar2023causalsim}.
Off-policy evaluation reweights logged rewards by the logged action probabilities, with doubly robust
variants~\cite{dudik2014doubly} and intervals under adaptive logging~\cite{hadad2021adaptive,zhang2021mestimators},
but it estimates the value of a policy, not the edges of a map. None of these methods tests a signed map of many
edges under a state-dependent policy.

Our conference work, CDD O-RAN, avoided confounding with an offline exploration phase in which a random policy
perturbed every control parameter~\cite{dahal2026cddoran}. It kept each edge whose smoothed conditional mutual
information (CMI) exceeded a threshold, so its graph was unsigned and without error control. The present method
draws its identification from operational accept-or-reject decisions instead.

\subsection{Conflict maps in O-RAN}
\label{sec:related_maps}

Existing conflict maps mostly predict KPIs or explain a fitted model. None that we found declares signed,
error-controlled effects of actions from logs whose actions the controller itself chose. Shapley additive
explanations (SHAP) attribute a model's prediction to its inputs~\cite{lundberg2017shap}, and their tree
form~\cite{lundberg2020trees} underlies our SHAP-GBDT baseline on gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDT). In O-RAN,
EXPLORA links a reinforcement-learning agent's actions to attributes of its input state~\cite{fiandrino2023explora},
and a survey covers explainable artificial intelligence for O-RAN control~\cite{brik2025xai}.

Rule-based conflict detectors take parameter groups configured by the operator~\cite{adamczyk2023conflict} or derived
from conflict graphs~\cite{wadud2025mobility}. Learned detectors use a graph convolutional network trained on
synthetic data~\cite{alshami2026conflict}, GraphSAGE reconstructions of conflict graphs~\cite{zolghadr2025conflict} or
a two-tower encoder of a sparse parameter-KPI graph~\cite{santos2026conflictairan}, the last two trained on data from
a known conflict model. XAI4C adds explainability-based detection and mitigation to the near-RT
RIC~\cite{varshney2025xai4c}, and PACIFISTA profiles each application in a sandbox to rate conflicts before
deployment~\cite{PACIFISTA}. The one causal approach, by Sharma et al., assembles a causal directed acyclic graph
(DAG) from a SHAP analysis and estimates signed average treatment effects~\cite{Sharma_2025}; its estimates are
identified only if that graph is correct and no confounder is left unmeasured. Our SHAP-GBDT baseline keeps only
their importance step.

\subsection{Safe conflict mitigation}
\label{sec:related_decisions}

Mitigation in the RIC takes the parameter-to-KPI relations as given or learns predictors of KPI levels, and safety
guarantees come from data on a whole policy or from a model of the system. None certifies decisions from the
uncertainty of a causal effect map. Once a conflict is found, mitigation decides what to
apply~\cite{ORANConflictMit,polese2023oran}: the framework of Adamczyk and Kliks gives priority to one of the
conflicting xApps~\cite{adamczyk2023conflict}, and quality-of-service-aware conflict mitigation (QACM) picks the
configuration that satisfies the most xApps, using regression predictors of their KPIs~\cite{QACM2024}. Other layers
let two xApps learn as a team~\cite{iturria2022matl}, train a scheduler that selects which xApps
act~\cite{cinemre2026scheduler}, or test conflicting actions in a network digital twin before applying
them~\cite{giannopoulos2025comix}.

Safe reinforcement learning (RL) keeps a learning controller away from harmful actions~\cite{gu2024safereview}, for
example by shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018shield}, and in RAN slicing its safety has been
shown empirically~\cite{nagib2024safe}. OpenTwin, a preprint, admits an xApp action only when a digital twin fitted
online places its outcome in the safe region, and bounds false approvals by an operator
budget~\cite{zhang2026opentwin}. High-confidence policy improvement bounds the probability that the returned policy
falls below a baseline~\cite{thomas2015hcpi}. Our edge-certification gate gives a weaker certificate, one per
undeclared guard edge, and claims no policy-level guarantee.
