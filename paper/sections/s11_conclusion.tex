\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper has shown that feedback-driven O-RAN logs can support causal claims about xApp conflicts, provided that the
conflict-mitigation layer randomises its accept-or-reject decisions and logs the probability of each. On such logs, a
test that redraws the logged decisions is valid for sharp nulls in large samples, without a model of the network. In a
simulated O-RAN scenario it declared no effect where none existed and recovered most of the true conflict edges,
whereas learned discovery methods declared effects on placebo data, and in controlled environments only tests that
used the logging design retained their nominal error rate. A valid map did not, however, improve conflict mitigation:
without a safety gate the map-driven referee breached a radio-link-failure limit, and with it the referee matched the
rule of never letting the pico cell sleep, as did a referee given the true map. In this scenario the safety guards,
rather than knowledge of the network, limited safe mitigation.

Three directions for future work follow. Guard-aware referees should be studied with a statistic for the guard edges
and a success criterion, such as energy retention at matched safety, fixed in advance. Further scenarios and incumbent policies would show
whether the equivalence with the never-sleep rule holds more generally. Finally, testbed logs that record their
propensities would test the logging requirement on which the approach rests.
