\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:design}
\label{sec:design_questions}

The framework is evaluated in two settings: a system-level simulation of an O-RAN surge scenario, which provides the
logs for discovery and the closed-loop runs for conflict mitigation, and controlled environments with known
causal structure, which isolate the role of the logging design. This section describes both settings, the datasets
drawn from them, the ground truth, and the baselines and metrics.

\subsection{Simulation environment and scenario}
\label{sec:scenario}\claim{DEF-SIM-CONST}

The simulator models a near-RT RIC and the RAN it controls (Figure~\ref{fig:system_e6p}). The network has 24 cells: 21
macro sectors on a seven-site hexagonal layout with wrap-around and an inter-site distance of 500~m~\cite{3gpp36814},
and three co-channel picos at traffic hotspots. Each of a fixed population of 300 UEs belongs to a low-latency (LL),
an enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB) or a best-effort slice. Traffic, mobility and a hidden per-site load process come from an
exogenous tape keyed by the seed, so that runs that act differently see the same tape; Appendix~\ref{app:simparams}
gives the radio, scheduling, energy and timing parameters. In the surge scenario, a localised surge triples the
file-arrival rate of the UEs within 250~m of a venue during the middle of the scored window, on a network whose
offered load would fill 40~\% of the PRBs with every xApp inactive.

Three xApps run on the RIC, each writing its own parameter families (Table~\ref{tab:xapps}). The Energy Saving (ES)
xApp switches macro carriers and puts picos to sleep using the load-time thresholds of 3GPP TS 28.541~\cite{3gpp28541},
and the Power ES xApp backs off the macro transmit power as in 3GPP TR 38.864~\cite{3gpp38864}. The Slice Guarantee
xApp sets the protected minimum PRB share that serves a protected subset of 20~\% of the eMBB UEs with a per-UE floor
of 2~Mb/s. The result is a three-way indirect conflict without a shared parameter: the energy-saving xApps act on the
KPIs of neighbouring cells, and Slice Guarantee on the protected-slice violations of its own cell. The chain of
interest runs from pico sleep to neighbour load and on to neighbour protected-slice and service violations, and the
edge from pico sleep to neighbour protected-slice violations is the one on which the conflict rests. Since the protected share
moves only its own cell's protected-slice violations, a referee that rejects Slice Guarantee's requests removes the
protection that the energy-saving xApps erode.

Five KPIs are recorded per cell. Protected-slice violations count violated protected-UE seconds, and service
violations count violated UE-seconds over all UEs, where an LL UE-second is violated above 100~ms head-of-line delay,
an eMBB UE-second when backlogged with a user-perceived throughput below 2~Mb/s, and any UE-second in RLF or out of
coverage. The other KPIs are energy, RLFs and served load in UE-seconds.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}\claim{PRO-TUNE}

Table~\ref{tab:design_studies} lists the datasets. Discovery is evaluated on randomised and on confounded logs, each
with 600 evaluation episodes and with placebo logs drawn in the same way, and validity is further checked on null
outcomes, made by cyclically shifting the KPI series across episodes. Null outcomes exercise more of the design than
placebo logs, because they keep the evaluation logs with their re-proposals and state-dependent propensities. The
maps learned from the confounded logs then drive referees in closed-loop runs on \val{s2.dec.seeds} fresh seeds, first without and
then with the gate; the gated runs use separate seeds and a gate calibrated on \val{s3.gate.dev_episodes} development episodes. The
controlled environments are described in Section~\ref{sec:envs}. All methods and baseline thresholds were tuned on
development episodes, separate from the evaluation data.

\subsection{Knockout ground truth}
\label{sec:gt}\claim{DEF-GT-LABEL}

The maps are scored against knockout rollouts, which estimate the effect of one unit's mode with every other unit
accepted. This target differs from PMRT's $\tau_{\pi}$, so the scores measure agreement between two related
quantities rather than the validity of the test.

\begin{definition}[Knockout effect]
\label{def:tau_aa}\claim{DEF-EST-TAUAA}
Let $u$ be a unit sampled on separate episodes. Its knockout effect $\tau_u^{\mathrm{AA}}$ is the accept-minus-reject
difference of the KPI summed over the cells of $r$ in the 90~s after opening, in rollouts from the copied state with
every other unit accepted. The knockout effect $\tau_{\mathrm{AA}}$ is the mean of $\mathrm{sgn}_u\,
\tau_u^{\mathrm{AA}}$ over the sampled units.
\end{definition}

Each sampled unit is rolled out for 90~s twice from its opening state, once accepted and once rejected, with the same
redrawn randomness, and the pair is repeated for three reseeds. Pooling the units of a family over \val{s1.gt.episodes} ground-truth episodes gives a mean
per hypothesis with a 95~\% episode-cluster bootstrap interval. A hypothesis is labelled
true, with the sign of its mean, when the interval excludes zero and the absolute mean is at least a materiality
threshold $\delta_k$. The threshold is 5~\% of the largest absolute mean of KPI $k$ over all families and relations, with a floor
of 0.5 violated UE-seconds for protected-slice violations. It is labelled null when the interval lies within
$\pm\delta_k$, and is otherwise left unscored, as it is when fewer than 10 units or 3 episodes support it. A knockout
null is therefore a small effect rather than a sharp null, and the labels depend materially on $\delta_k$, though hardly
on the floor or the interval level (Appendix~\ref{app:x11}). Table~\ref{tab:system_hypotheses} gives the labels for the
randomised logs.

\subsection{Controlled environments}
\label{sec:envs}\claim{DEF-ENV}\claim{DEF-ENV-ARMS}\claim{ASM-S4-KNOWN}
\label{sec:study4_design}
\label{sec:study4_purpose}

The simulator runs one scenario with one logging design per dataset, and its ground truth is estimated. The controlled
environments remove both limitations: each isolates one mechanism with a known causal graph, so that every null edge
is null by construction, and the same mechanism is logged under several designs. The five environments contain a
clean linear graph, an edge active only in a rare regime, delayed harm through lagged KPI cascades, latent
confounding, and the three mechanisms composed (Figure~\ref{fig:envs}a; Appendix~\ref{app:envs}). Actions are logged in one of three ways:
independent redraws per observation; a dithered setpoint, with 20 setpoints each held for a twentieth of the data plus
a small independent dither; and, in the latent-confounding environment, a confounded behaviour policy with logged
probabilities (Figure~\ref{fig:envs}b). Each method ran at 500 to 24\,000 observations, and each condition on 40 seeds, which suffices to put an
interval on a null-edge rate.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_envs.pdf}
\caption{Controlled environments and logging designs.}

Three groups of tests are compared. Design-based tests draw their p-value by redrawing the logged design; they are
PMRT-GBM, the shared construction with a gradient-boosting statistic and plain BY control at $q = 0.05$, and PMRT-Lin,
its linear counterpart. Design-adjusted tests are standard conditional-independence tests with the logged design
variables as covariates: partial correlation, Granger and RCoT~\cite{strobl2019rcot}. Design-blind methods ignore the
design: correlation, plain partial correlation, also with heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent
errors~\cite{newey1987hac,kiefer2005fixedb}, plain Granger and RCoT, and the structure learners PC~\cite{spirtes2000causation}
and NOTEARS~\cite{zheng2018notears}. The design-blind group also includes SHAP-DAG after Sharma et al.~\cite{Sharma_2025,lundberg2017shap}, the two-tower
network, the max-stratified correlation-ratio test of our earlier work, and the discovery stage of CDD
O-RAN~\cite{dahal2026cddoran}. The controlled environments test the randomisation construction that PMRT shares, not
its matched-filter statistic or its weighted declaration layer, and both PMRT variants there omit the Huber clip of the
unit weight, which raised the null rate on development data. The validity of the PMRT used in the simulator therefore
rests on its own null checks there (Section~\ref{sec:res_validity}).

\subsection{Baselines and metrics}
\label{sec:baselines_scores}\claim{DEF-MET-MAP}\claim{DEF-MET-DEC}
\label{sec:arms}

In the simulator, PMRT is compared with seven associational baselines that ignore the logging design and run on the
same unit table (Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}). They are correlation, Granger~\cite{granger1969causal} with a tuned
threshold or under BY control (Granger~+~BY), SHAP-GBDT~\cite{Sharma_2025}, a two-tower
network~\cite{santos2026conflictairan}, the intent-conflict statistic INT of PACIFISTA~\cite{PACIFISTA} and
QACM~\cite{QACM2024}. Each threshold
was set on development episodes so that the far relation received at most one declaration. Because the ground truth
labels \val{s1.far.true} of the \val{s1.far.total} far hypotheses true on the randomised logs and \val{s2.far.true} on the confounded logs, this rule caps the recall
a baseline can reach. On the randomised logs the modes are independent of the network state, so the baselines'
placebo declarations there come from their thresholds rather than from confounding. On the confounded logs the
baselines are also reported at thresholds calibrated on placebo logs.

Maps are scored over the \val{s1.gt.hypotheses} hypotheses. Hypotheses whose ground-truth label is indeterminate are not scored, so a declaration on one counts in neither measure. Precision is the share of declarations on determinate hypotheses that are true, whatever their
sign; recall is the share of true hypotheses that are declared; F1 is their harmonic mean; and sign accuracy is the
share of declared true edges that carry the ground-truth sign. Referees are compared with static rules, such as
accept-all, never-sleep, which rejects every request to put a pico to sleep, and the subsets of xApps run alone. They are also compared
with referees driven by the oracle map of true edges, a random map and the associational maps
(Appendix~\ref{app:arms}). The objective is the protected-slice violations $V$, reported as the recovery
$R = (V_{\mathrm{AA}} - V) / (V_{\mathrm{AA}} - V_{\mathrm{ref}})$, which is 0 for accept-all and 1 for the best
single-xApp subset. A referee is eligible when it meets the two constraints of Section~\ref{sec:estimand}: an energy
retention of at least 0.90 and four guard ratios to accept-all of at most 1.10. Never-sleep is the best eligible static
rule, because the subset that sets $R = 1$ retains almost none of the energy saving. Intervals on decision metrics are
one-sided or two-sided 90~\% paired seed-bootstrap intervals with 10\,000 resamples, and intervals on rates are 95~\%
cluster-bootstrap intervals. Online Resource~1, Section~S1, gives the pass criteria of each dataset and their
results.\label{sec:protocol}
