\subsection{Validity}
\label{sec:res_validity}\claim{EMP-S1-PLACEBO}\claim{EMP-S2-PLACEBO}\claim{EMP-S1-NULLOUT}\claim{EMP-S1-STRICT}\claim{EMP-S1-SMALLNULL}\claim{EMP-BASE-MAPS}

PMRT made no declaration on either dataset's placebo logs or on the null outcomes, whose p-values were close to
uniform (\esm{esm:discovery}). The baselines declared placebo edges under both logging designs
(Figure~\ref{fig:placebo}). Under uniform randomisation these arise from their thresholds rather than confounding;
under state-dependent logging, four of seven baselines declared more, consistent with confounding adding to that
effect. Knockout-null labels permit small nonzero effects, so their raw p-value rate need not equal the sharp-null
level.

\subsection{Agreement with the ground truth}
\label{sec:res_map}\claim{EMP-S1-MAP}\claim{EMP-S2-MAP}\claim{EMP-S1-CHAIN}\claim{EMP-S1-P2}\claim{EMP-GT-SENS}\claim{EMP-GT-EXIST}

PMRT's maps agreed closely with the knockout ground truth on both logging designs (Figure~\ref{fig:maps},
Table~\ref{tab:maps}). It recovered all four edges of the scenario's conflict chain with the ground-truth signs and
made no wrong-sign or placebo declaration. The baselines either recovered fewer true edges or gained recall at the
cost of wrong signs or placebo edges. Granger~+~BY had the strongest recall among them, while SHAP-GBDT avoided wrong
signs but declared placebo edges. Calibrating the baseline thresholds on the placebo logs themselves still left one
placebo edge in every map.

On the smallest subsamples of the uniform-randomised logs, PMRT's F1 score on neighbour-relation edges did not consistently
match that of Granger~+~BY (\esm{fig:s_subsamples}).

Precision changed with the materiality threshold because some declared effects fell below the stricter threshold,
not because their signs were wrong. Under an existence rule, which counts every effect whose knockout interval
excludes zero, \val{s1.ex.confirmed} of PMRT's \val{s1.pmrt.declared} declarations on the uniform-randomised logs and
all \val{s2.ex.confirmed} on the state-dependent logs were confirmed with the declared sign. PMRT made no wrong-sign
declaration under any ground-truth variant, although doubling the threshold changed the ranking of methods
(\esm{esm:discovery}).

\subsection{Role of the logging design}
\label{sec:study4}\claim{EMP-S4-C1}\claim{EMP-S4-C2C3}\claim{EMP-S4-E4}\claim{EMP-S4-CDD}\claim{EMP-POWER}

The controlled environments confirm that validity derives from the logging design. Tests that ignored the design
were near nominal under independent logging but lost validity once actions followed a dithered setpoint, whereas the
design-based and design-adjusted tests remained near 0.05 under every logging design
(Figure~\ref{fig:logging_validity}; \esm{esm:envs}). Two exceptions stand out:
design-adjusted RCoT was invalid even with the design covariates, and PMRT-Lin, the linear variant, was invalid in
some conditions under the state-dependent policy, although its pooled rates were valid.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_logging_validity.pdf}
\caption{Null-edge rate by logging design.}
\label{fig:logging_validity}
\end{figure}

The latent-confounding environment shows the mechanism directly: strong confounding reverses the associational sign.
Under the logged state-dependent policy, PMRT-GBM and design-adjusted partial correlation remained valid, whereas
correlation and plain RCoT were far above nominal. Varying the dither width and number of setpoints left correlation
invalid and both PMRT methods valid.

Power, unlike validity, differed between the valid tests. In the latent-confounding environment, design-adjusted
partial correlation had higher recall than PMRT-GBM at the largest sample size (\esm{fig:s_recall}); on the
uniform-randomised simulator logs, PMRT had higher pooled recall than either partial-correlation variant
(\esm{tab:s_bridge}).

The same environments show the limits of a placebo check. CDD O-RAN's discovery stage passed after calibration on the
placebo column but still exceeded the nominal null-edge rate in some conditions. Declaring placebo edges therefore
reveals invalidity, but passing the check does not establish validity: it is necessary, not sufficient.

\subsection{Conflict mitigation}
\label{sec:res_decision}\claim{EMP-S2-REFEREE}\claim{EMP-S2-MECH}\claim{EMP-S2-ASSOC}\claim{EMP-S3-GATED}\claim{EMP-S3-ORACLE}\claim{EMP-X15}

Without the gate, the PMRT referee reached the highest recovery but breached the 1.10 RLF limit and was therefore
ineligible (Figure~\ref{fig:decisions}, Table~\ref{tab:decision_e2e}). The breach came from rejected transmit-power
requests. PMRT's map lacked the effect of the transmit-power offset on neighbour RLFs, whereas the oracle map contained
it and consequently deferred more power-up rejections; their sleep rejections were similar (\esm{fig:s_deferrals}).

With the gate, the PMRT referee met every guard but made the same decisions as never-sleep on every seed. No gated
referee improved on that rule, including the referee given the oracle map. The ungated PMRT result varied on the fresh
seed block; pooled over both blocks, its gain over never-sleep was small and its RLF ratio still breached the guard,
indicating seed-level heterogeneity of the adaptive referee (\esm{esm:decision}). Referees using the associational
maps raised protected-slice violations above accept-all with and without the gate because they rejected Slice
Guarantee's protected-share requests; several stayed eligible because the protected slice has no guard of its own
(\esm{tab:s_x7}).

\label{sec:disc_decision}\claim{EMP-S3-GATEDIAG}\claim{EMP-X4}
The gate accounts for the absence of a gain from a valid map. Of the request classes that the PMRT map allows the
referee to reject, the gate certified only putting picos to sleep. It withheld the transmit-power-up rejections because
their two unresolved guard edges, to neighbour RLFs and far service violations, are true in the ground truth, with
point estimates of harm above their tolerances, so that more logging would not have certified them. The oracle map
certifies this class only because it declares both edges, and its gated referee gained over never-sleep only in energy
retention (\esm{fig:s_cert}). Rejecting more requests does not
compensate for this: a referee that rejected requests at random, at the rate of the ungated referee, had negative
recovery (\esm{tab:s_arms2}). In this scenario,
therefore, the guards and the objective, rather than the map, limited the benefit of safe decisions.
