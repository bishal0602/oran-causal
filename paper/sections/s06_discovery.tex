\subsection{Validity}
\label{sec:res_validity}\claim{EMP-S1-PLACEBO}\claim{EMP-S2-PLACEBO}\claim{EMP-S1-NULLOUT}\claim{EMP-S1-STRICT}\claim{EMP-S1-SMALLNULL}\claim{EMP-BASE-MAPS}

PMRT made no declaration on the placebo logs of either dataset or on the null outcomes, whose p-values were close to
uniform (\esm{esm:discovery}). The baselines, in contrast, declared edges on placebo logs, up to \val{s1.placebo.granger_by}
on the uniform-randomised logs and up to \val{s2.placebo.granger_by} on the state-dependent logs (Figure~\ref{fig:placebo}). Because
the modes of the uniform-randomised logs are independent of the network state, the declarations there arise from the baselines'
thresholds rather than from confounding; on the state-dependent logs, four of the seven baselines declared more placebo
edges, which is consistent with confounding adding to the threshold effect. Because knockout-null labels permit small
nonzero effects, their raw p-value rate need not equal the sharp-null level; over all \val{s1.null.count}, the rate at
or below 0.05 was \val{s1.null.rate}.

\subsection{Agreement with the ground truth}
\label{sec:res_map}\claim{EMP-S1-MAP}\claim{EMP-S2-MAP}\claim{EMP-S1-CHAIN}\claim{EMP-S1-P2}\claim{EMP-GT-SENS}\claim{EMP-GT-EXIST}

PMRT's maps agreed closely with the knockout ground truth (Figure~\ref{fig:maps}, Table~\ref{tab:maps}). On the
uniform-randomised logs it declared \val{s1.pmrt.declared} edges, with a precision of \val{s1.map.pmrt.prec} over the
determinate hypotheses, a recall of \val{s1.map.pmrt.rec} and no wrong sign. On the state-dependent logs, where the
incumbent's state-dependent propensities were logged, it declared \val{s2.pmrt.declared} edges, with a precision of
\val{s2.map.pmrt.prec}, a recall of \val{s2.map.pmrt.rec} and again no wrong sign. It declared the edge from pico sleep
to neighbour protected-slice violations, on which the scenario's conflict rests, and the other three edges of the
conflict chain, each with the ground-truth sign, on both kinds of log and in every larger subsample of the uniform-randomised
logs. The baselines either declared fewer true edges or reached a higher recall only with wrong signs and false edges.
Granger~+~BY, the strongest of them, reached a recall of \val{s1.map.gby.rec} with six wrong signs on each kind of
log. SHAP-GBDT declared no wrong sign, but it declared \val{s1.map.shap.placebo} edges on the placebo logs of the
uniform-randomised dataset. Calibrating the baselines' thresholds on the placebo logs themselves still left a placebo edge in
every map, and no calibrated map reached a recall above \val{s2.plc.rec_max}, wrong signs included.

On the smallest subsamples of the uniform-randomised logs, PMRT's F1 score on neighbour-relation edges did not consistently
match that of Granger~+~BY (\esm{fig:s_subsamples}).

Precision depends on how the ground truth is defined. With the materiality threshold halved it rose to
\val{s1.gt.half.prec} on both kinds of log, and with it doubled it fell to \val{s1.gt.dbl.pmrt.prec} and
\val{s2.gt.dbl.pmrt.prec}, because of real effects below the doubled threshold rather than wrong declarations. Under an
existence rule, which counts any effect whose knockout interval excludes zero, \val{s1.ex.confirmed} of PMRT's
\val{s1.pmrt.declared} declarations on the uniform-randomised logs and all \val{s2.ex.confirmed} on the state-dependent logs were
real effects with the declared sign. PMRT made no wrong-sign declaration under any ground-truth variant
(\esm{esm:discovery}), but under the doubled threshold the ranking of methods is not preserved: the small maps of INT,
QACM and two-tower kept higher precision at lower recall.

\subsection{Role of the logging design}
\label{sec:study4}\claim{EMP-S4-C1}\claim{EMP-S4-C2C3}\claim{EMP-S4-E4}\claim{EMP-S4-CDD}\claim{EMP-POWER}

The controlled environments confirm that validity derives from the logging design. Tests that ignored the design
were close to their nominal rate under independent logging but lost it once actions followed a dithered setpoint, with
null-edge rates of \val{s4.c1.r2_min} to \val{s4.c1.r2_max}, whereas the design-based and design-adjusted tests remained
near 0.05 under every logging design (Figure~\ref{fig:logging_validity}; \esm{esm:envs}). Two exceptions stand out:
design-adjusted RCoT was invalid even with the design covariates, and PMRT-Lin, the linear variant, was invalid in
some conditions under the state-dependent policy, although its pooled rates were valid.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_logging_validity.pdf}
\caption{Null-edge rate by logging design.}
\label{fig:logging_validity}
\end{figure}

The latent-confounding environment illustrates the mechanism most directly, because there the associational sign is
reversed at large confounding strength. Under the logged state-dependent policy, the null-edge rate of correlation was
\val{s4.conf.corr_min} whenever the confounding was present, while that of PMRT-GBM stayed between
\val{s4.conf.gbm_min} and \val{s4.conf.gbm_max} in every condition. Validity under this policy is not specific to PMRT:
design-adjusted partial correlation also stayed valid, whereas correlation and plain RCoT were far above nominal, at
\val{s4.conf.corr_all} and \val{s4.conf.rcot2_all}. A variation of the dither width and of the number of setpoints left
correlation invalid and the two PMRT methods between \val{s4.dither.min} and \val{s4.dither.max}.

Power, unlike validity, differed between the valid tests. In the latent-confounding environment, which has a single true
edge, the recall of PMRT-GBM did not exceed \val{s4.e4.pmrtgbm.recall_max} at any sample size, whereas design-adjusted
partial correlation reached \val{s4.e4.pcorr_eq.recall_24k} at the largest (\esm{fig:s_recall}). On the uniform-randomised
simulator logs, which contain no confounding, the pooled recalls were \val{br.pcorr_eq.recall} for design-adjusted
partial correlation, without a placebo declaration, \val{br.pcorr_native.recall} for partial correlation with context
covariates alone, and \val{s1.map.pmrt.rec} for PMRT (\esm{tab:s_bridge}).

The same environments show the limits of a placebo check. With its threshold calibrated on the placebo column, the
discovery stage of CDD O-RAN passed that check but exceeded the nominal null-edge rate in \val{s4.cdl.invalid} of
\val{s4.cdl.conditions} conditions. A pipeline that declares placebo edges, as every baseline did on the uniform-randomised
logs, is thus shown to be invalid, whereas a pipeline that passes the check may still be invalid: the check is a
necessary condition for validity, but not a sufficient one.

\subsection{Conflict mitigation}
\label{sec:res_decision}\claim{EMP-S2-REFEREE}\claim{EMP-S2-MECH}\claim{EMP-S2-ASSOC}\claim{EMP-S3-GATED}\claim{EMP-S3-ORACLE}\claim{EMP-X15}

Without the gate, the PMRT referee reached the highest recovery of any referee, $R = \val{s2.dec.pmrt.rcv}$, but
breached the RLF guard and was therefore ineligible (Figure~\ref{fig:decisions}, Table~\ref{tab:decision_e2e}). Its RLF
ratio was \val{s2.dec.pmrt.rlf} (90~\% confidence interval, CI, \val{s2.dec.pmrt.rlf_lo} to
\val{s2.dec.pmrt.rlf_hi}), against the limit of 1.10. The breach came from rejected transmit-power requests. PMRT's
map from the state-dependent logs lacked the edge from the transmit-power offset to neighbour RLFs, which the ground truth
labels true; the oracle map holds this edge, so its guard-conflict rule accepts the next power-up unit after each
rejected one. Over all seeds, PMRT's referee rejected \val{s2.defer.ptx_up.pmrt} power-up units against the oracle
map's \val{s2.defer.ptx_up.gt}, while their sleep rejections were similar (\esm{fig:s_deferrals}).

With the gate, the PMRT referee met every guard but made the same decisions as the never-sleep rule on every seed, so
its gain in recovery over that rule was zero. No gated referee improved on never-sleep; the oracle map differed from it
by \val{s3.dec.gt.dr} (90~\% CI \val{s3.dec.gt.dr_lo} to \val{s3.dec.gt.dr_hi}). The gated runs used fresh seeds, and
on them the ungated PMRT referee no longer outperformed never-sleep, differing by \val{s3.mgpmrt.dr} at an RLF ratio
of \val{s3.mgpmrt.rlf}. Pooled over both seed sets, its gain was small, \val{x15.pooled.dr} with an interval that
barely excludes zero, and its RLF ratio of \val{x15.pooled.rlf} still breached the guard; we attribute the difference
between the seed sets to seed-level heterogeneity of an adaptive referee (\esm{esm:decision}). Referees using the
associational maps raised protected-slice violations to \val{s2.assoc.ratio_min} to \val{s2.assoc.ratio_max} times the
accept-all level with and without the gate, because they rejected Slice Guarantee's protected-share requests; several
stayed eligible, since the protected slice has no guard of its own (\esm{tab:s_x7}).

\label{sec:disc_decision}\claim{EMP-S3-GATEDIAG}\claim{EMP-X4}
The gate accounts for the absence of a gain from a valid map. Of the request classes that the PMRT map allows the
referee to reject, the gate certified only putting picos to sleep. It withheld the transmit-power-up rejections because
their two unresolved guard edges, to neighbour RLFs and far service violations, are true in the ground truth, with
point estimates of harm above their tolerances, so that more logging would not have certified them. The oracle map
certifies this class only because it declares both edges, and its gated referee gained over never-sleep only in energy
retention, \val{s3.dec.gt.ret} against \val{s3.dec.ns.ret} (\esm{fig:s_cert}). Rejecting more requests does not
compensate for this: a referee that rejected requests at random, at the rate of the ungated referee, had negative
recovery (\esm{tab:s_arms2}). In this scenario,
therefore, the guards and the objective, rather than the map, limited the benefit of safe decisions.
