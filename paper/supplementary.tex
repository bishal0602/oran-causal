\subsection{Criteria and verdicts}
\label{esm:criteria}

For each dataset of the simulator, the criteria below were fixed before the evaluation data were generated
(Section~5.6 of the article). Tables~\ref{tab:s_crit1} to~\ref{tab:s_crit3} give each criterion, its rule, the measured
value and the result. A dataset's verdict follows a fixed precedence: a failed placebo or null-outcome check makes it
\verdict{invalid}, a ground truth without the edge from pico sleep to neighbour protected-slice violations gives \verdict{no-chain}, insufficient support gives
\verdict{underpowered}, and otherwise the verdict is \verdict{pass} when every remaining criterion holds,
\verdict{partial} when the pooled criteria hold, and \verdict{fail} or \verdict{kill} otherwise. A referee that misses
the eligibility limits is \verdict{not eligible} before any margin is read. The effect of pico sleep on neighbour protected-slice violations is in violated
protected-UE seconds over the neighbour cells in the 90~s after the request, with a 95~\% episode-cluster bootstrap
interval; referee intervals are 90~\% paired seed-bootstrap intervals.

\subsection{Status of analyses}
\label{esm:status}

Table~\ref{tab:s_status} lists every analysis that is not a protocol criterion, where it is reported, its status and
the date on which it was declared in the project's protocol log. A secondary analysis was specified in a protocol but
not scored; a post hoc analysis was chosen after the protocol was fixed. Every post hoc analysis was declared before it
was run except two: the look at the unfiltered statistic in Section~6.4 of the article, and the reading of the ungated
referee's replication in Section~6.4 of the article. No post hoc analysis changes a verdict, and a descriptive analysis carries no
verdict of its own. One amendment, A-1, changed the fixed code of the controlled environments: it altered only the job
dispatcher, and every evaluation record of those environments was produced under it. The referee evaluations ran in a
fixed order, development runs whose failed checks would have stopped the line, then the referee without the gate, then
the referee with the gate, after which a stop rule ended the line; no other referee evaluation was run.

\subsection{Existence ground truth}
\label{esm:x14}

Under the existence rule, an effect exists when the 95~\% episode-cluster interval of the knockout ground truth
excludes zero, whatever its size. A declared edge is confirmed when an effect exists with the declared sign, wrong-signed
when it exists with the other sign, and not established otherwise (Table~\ref{tab:s_exist}). Every true hypothesis of
the materiality rule has an effect by construction. PMRT's two unconfirmed declarations on the randomised logs,
transmit power on far protected-slice violations and pico sleep on own protected-slice violations, are indeterminate
under the materiality rule as well, while three of its declarations that the materiality rule labels null or
indeterminate have an effect with the declared sign, and all four such declarations do on the confounded logs. The rule
was declared after the sensitivity analysis had been seen, and because it uses intervals from 40 ground-truth episodes,
it measures detectability in this ground truth rather than practical relevance.

\subsection{Small-effect nulls}
\label{esm:expd}

Section~6.1 of the article reports how often the hypotheses that the ground truth labels null reach $p \le 0.05$. The
rates were recomputed from the stored per-subsample p-values of PMRT's primary statistic, which reproduced 1\,020
stored hypothesis tables without a mismatch. On the 12 null hypotheses whose interval also contains zero, the rate was
0.075 at 60 episodes and 0.083 at 120; intervals that resample hypotheses and subsamples together run from 0.017 to
0.158 and from 0 to 0.217, and the 120-episode subsamples re-partition the same episodes, so the two rates are not
independent confirmations. The two null hypotheses rejected most often, pico sleep on own-cell energy and macro carrier
on own-cell load, were rejected in 10 and 8 of the 10 subsamples of 60 episodes, always with the ground-truth sign;
their knockout intervals exclude zero, so they are real effects below $\delta_k$. One rejection, in one subsample,
carried the wrong sign on a real far-relation energy effect of the macro carrier below $\delta_k$. Without the Huber
clip, the rate on all 21 null hypotheses was 0.157 instead of 0.152.

\section{Controlled environments}
\label{esm:envs}

\subsection{Validity rule and claims}
\label{esm:rule}

A condition is one environment, logging design, confounding strength $\lambda$ and sample size. Its rates are the
shares of truth-null, placebo and confounded-null edges with $p \le 0.05$, and the share declared under BY. A rate is
\verdict{invalid} if the lower bound of its 95~\% seed-cluster bootstrap interval exceeds 0.05, \verdict{valid} if the
upper bound is at most 0.075, and \verdict{inconclusive} otherwise, and a condition is \verdict{invalid} if any of its
rates is. Because an exactly nominal test also produces \verdict{invalid} conditions by chance, each method's count of
\verdict{invalid} conditions is compared with $F_{\max}$, the count that such a test stays within with probability
0.95 under the estimated dependence between conditions: 3 for the 50 conditions under independent and dithered logging,
2 for the 20 under the confounded policy and 1 for the 10 of the delayed-harm environment.

The protocol fixed four claims (Table~\ref{tab:s_claims}). A design-blind method fails as the first claim predicts when
at least half of its dithered-setpoint conditions are \verdict{invalid}, its pooled truth-null or placebo rate under
that design is \verdict{invalid}, and its independent-logging conditions stay within $F_{\max}$; a method that exceeds
$F_{\max}$ already under independent logging is invalid for another reason and counts neither way. The first claim is
\verdict{supported} when at least half of the design-blind methods fail in this way. Each member of the other three
claims must keep its \verdict{invalid} conditions within $F_{\max}$ and its pooled rates \verdict{valid}. The third
claim covers the design-adjusted version of each test that the first claim finds invalid, except MSCR, whose
single-conditioner statistic cannot condition on the joint design set, and it is \verdict{supported} when at least half
of its members pass. The overall claim is \verdict{supported} only when all four are, and recall is read only among
conditions that are not \verdict{invalid}.

The verdicts carry the following exceptions. Design-adjusted RCoT was \verdict{invalid} under dithered logging, at 0.292
(0.283 to 0.301), and already under independent logging, at 0.061 (0.056 to 0.066), so it is outside the third claim;
plain RCoT had 6 of 25 independent-logging conditions \verdict{invalid} against $F_{\max}$ 2 and counts neither for nor
against the first. All six design-blind tests were \verdict{invalid} under dithered logging, five of them as the first
claim predicts, and without MSCR the claim still holds, with four of five. The fourth claim is not specific to PMRT:
design-adjusted partial correlation was also supported, with 2 \verdict{invalid} conditions and placebo and
confounded-null rates of 0.056 and 0.058, while RCoT (17 of 20 \verdict{invalid}), PC (7 of 20) and MSCR (6 of 8) with
design covariates do not bear on it. PMRT-GBM had 1 \verdict{invalid} condition, within $F_{\max}$ 3, and PMRT-Lin none,
with pooled rates of 0.049 and 0.050. The first claim holds in every environment except latent confounding, which has no
real-action null edge and is judged on a placebo edge, where 2 of 22 design-blind conditions were \verdict{invalid}.
PMRT-Lin was \verdict{not supported} under the confounded policy, with 3 \verdict{invalid} conditions against
$F_{\max}$ 2, although its pooled rates of 0.051 and 0.049 were \verdict{valid}; a re-run on fresh data gave rates
consistent with chance (Section~\ref{esm:rerun}). Under the confounded policy with $\lambda > 0$, the confounded-null
rate of correlation was 1.00 in every condition, that of PMRT-GBM stayed between 0.02 and 0.06, and that of
design-adjusted partial correlation between 0.03 and 0.09.

Both PMRT variants of these environments omit the Huber clip of the unit weight that the PMRT of the simulator uses. On
development data with the clip at 2.5, the null rate on the rare-regime truth-null edges under dithered logging at
$n = 1\,000$ was 0.070 (0.055 to 0.087) at level 0.05 and 0.020 at level 0.01; without the clip it was 0.052 (0.037 to
0.067) and 0.009, and at $n = 4\,000$ the excess had gone, at 0.045 and 0.005. The redraws hold the weights fixed,
clip scale included, but here the weights depend on the action's own earlier draws, so validity rests on the normal
limit, and at $n = 1\,000$ the clipped statistic's null $z$ was over-dispersed. Under unlogged confounding, the real
actions have no logged design, so the design-based tests were read on the placebo edge only, in 20 conditions per
method, and none was \verdict{invalid}: PMRT-GBM had 6 \verdict{valid} and 14 \verdict{inconclusive} conditions,
PMRT-Lin 3 and 17, and PMRT-Lin with confounder covariates 4 and 16.

\subsection{Design misspecification}
\label{esm:misspec}

Here the data kept the true design and the method was told a wrong one, at $n = 1\,000$ on 60 seeds. On the
truth-null edges of the linear and rare-regime environments under dithered logging, the method was told a dither width
of 0.5 to 2 times the truth or setpoint changes 2 or 5~\% of a block late; on the confounded null edge of the
latent-confounding environment under the confounded policy, it was told a policy strength $\lambda$ of 0.5 to 2 times
the truth (Figure~\ref{fig:s_misspec} and Table~\ref{tab:s_misspec}). PMRT-GBM and PMRT-Lin were \verdict{invalid} when
told 0.5 or 0.8 times the true width, PMRT-Lin from a 2~\% delay and PMRT-GBM at 5~\% and at twice the true width, and
every PMRT variant when told twice the true $\lambda$. Design-adjusted partial correlation was \verdict{invalid} only at
the 5~\% delay. These results show where the known-design assumption breaks; they are not evidence against validity
under a known design.

\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_appC_x3_misspec.pdf}

\subsection{Centring ablation}
\label{esm:centring}

PMRT-GBM became \verdict{invalid} when told a dither twice the true width (Section~\ref{esm:misspec}). A $2 \times 2$
ablation in the linear environment under dithered logging at $n = 1\,000$, on 200 fresh data sets, separated the law
used to centre the statistic from the law used for the redraws (Table~\ref{tab:s_centring}). With the told law
throughout, the truth-null rate was 0.079 (0.068 to 0.090); with true-law centring and told-law redraws it was 0.038
(0.031 to 0.047), with told centring and true redraws 0.046, and with the exact design 0.043, so the inflation came from
the centring rather than from the redraws. Over a grid of told widths of 0.5, 0.8, 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2 times the truth,
PMRT-GBM's rate was 0.156, 0.083, 0.043, 0.038, 0.048 and 0.079, and PMRT-Lin's 0.322, 0.112, 0.052, 0.012, 0.003 and
0.000. The first-order bias term does not explain the size of the inflation, with means of 0.090 under told centring
and $-0.448$ under true centring against rates of 0.079 and 0.038, so the mechanism remains open.

\section{Bridging analysis and computing time}
\label{esm:bridge}

\subsection{Bridging analysis}
\label{esm:bridging}

The bridging analysis is post hoc, declared before it was run, and descriptive. It ran the methods of the controlled
environments on the randomised simulator logs, with the PMRT of the simulator as a reference row rather than a
competitor. Each row of a data set is one randomised decision unit of one parameter family, in information order. The
action is the logged mode, 1 for accept and 0 for reject, times the request's direction, so it follows the logged draw;
the design is the logged propensity, 0.5, so a design-based test redraws the randomisation that PMRT redraws; and the
placebo column is drawn independently from the same design. The outcomes are the 15 targets of the article, three
relations by five KPIs, summed over the 90~s after the unit opens, with the sum over the 90~s before it and PMRT's
observation-only context as covariates. Data sets cover the 600 evaluation episodes pooled, ten subsamples of 60, five
of 120 and two of 300 episodes, and the 40 placebo episodes, per family.

Table~\ref{tab:s_bridge} gives every method by subsample. Recall is over all 29 true edges of the own, neighbour and
far relations; over the 10 neighbour-relation edges alone, PMRT's pooled recall is 0.90. The covariate-adjusted linear
methods, PMRT-Lin, design-adjusted partial correlation and Granger, and the HAC partial correlation with context
covariates, reached a recall of 0.49 to 0.52 at 60 episodes and 0.69 to 0.72 pooled, with null rates of 0.05 to 0.10
and no declaration on the placebo logs; correlation and plain Granger declared on 0.59 and 0.40 of the placebo-column
tests at 60 episodes. On these logs, whose design is the constant 0.5, the plain partial correlation came close to the
design-adjusted one, at 0.69 against 0.72 pooled, so the analysis measures power rather than the source of validity.
PMRT-GBM reached 0.39 to 0.55, with null rates of 0.05 to 0.14, and the PMRT of the simulator, helped by weights fitted
on earlier episodes, 0.79. Of 1\,328 method-data-set analyses, 12 were infeasible within the budget of 7\,200 CPU-s,
all for CDD O-RAN discovery (Table~\ref{tab:s_cost}). Intervals over 10 or 5 subsamples are not a precision statement
and are not shown.
